    Chapter 16 — Glossary¶

    Every term this manual uses without stopping to explain it, with the chapter or step where it first matters. Ch 00 Steps 00.23–00.29 teach the fastener and drawing vocabulary once and then rely on it for ~600 steps; this page makes that reliance recoverable instead of assumed.

    Time: none — reference page.

    Prerequisites: none. Tools: none. Printed parts: none. Hardware: none.

    Read first

    • One line each, and the link is the real answer. Where a definition and a step disagree, the step wins.
    • Symbols first, then A–Z. Document names are folded into the alphabet under their own initial (LDO Build Notes under L, Voron Assembly Manual under V).
    • "First matters at" is the earliest place in this manual where you must already understand the term — not necessarily where the word first appears.

    Symbols, prefixes and markers¶

    Term What it means First matters at
    [a]_ STL filename prefix: print in the accent colour — ASA Blue on this build. Every [a]_ plate part is collected into batch B02; the add-ons' [a]_ parts print with their add-on, outside the plates 00.24
    [o]_ Prefix: opaque — the part must block light. Galaxy Black satisfies it print/00-slicer-setup — Colour key
    [c]_ Prefix: clear / translucent. Do not print — LDO supplies the one such part (the SB LED diffuser) in clear PETG 00.6
    _xN Filename suffix: the quantity the machine needs, regardless of how many bodies are in the file. _x4 means four 00.24
    B00–B13 Print-batch ids, always two digits: B00–B10, B12 and B13 are the ASA run (B12 and B13 the add-ons), B11 the PETG V0 bay ducts. A plate inside a batch is B08-P4 — two-digit batch, unpadded plate number 00.11
    p.NN A page in the official Voron 2.4r2 Assembly Manual PDF, which has not changed since 2023-07-18 — the page numbers do not move 00.29
    nhk: Klipper pin prefix for the toolboard MCU ([mcu nhk]). A nhk: pin is on the Nitehawk, not the Leviathan 12.21
    ! (leading, on a pin) Klipper's invert marker. On an endstop it is almost always the wrong fix — stock Voron endstops are normally-closed to ground 13.17
    OL Multimeter display for open / over-range — no measurable path. The expected reading for L or N to PE and for the bed heater to the plate. Not for the 24 V side: the ESD bond puts −V on PE, so −V reads a few Ω and +V about the +V → −V figure (10.74) 10.13
    2020 extrusion The aluminium bar the frame is made of: 20 × 20 mm, with a T-slot down each face. The kit names its lengths A to E 01.2
    Plate mark The plate id and the date, written in paint marker on a non-visible face of every part as it is sorted. A reprint adds R and the extrusion multiplier if it changed print/README § Bins
    (verify on bench) This manual's marker for a count, torque or dimension no source publishes. Measure it yourself; never invent it CONVENTIONS
    Source: The line closing every step, linking the exact page or guide section that step was transcribed from CONVENTIONS
    Pause: A safe stopping point, with the minutes since the last one and the state you leave the machine in CONVENTIONS
    The raven The manual's mascot, named Revali (see the mascot page); voron is Russian for raven. As a 30 px badge it marks a Check, a Tip, a Pause:, a Gather block or a **Helper:** line; larger, it is a captioned aside, a gate's PASS/FAIL verdict, or the bird on Home. It never appears on a ⚠ box, and never on mains, iron, blade or hot-chamber work CONVENTIONS — Interactive fences

    Source: Ch 00 Step 00.24 · print/00-slicer-setup — Colour key · docs/manual/CONVENTIONS.md

    A¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    A and B The two CoreXY motors and their belts. A is the right-hand drive, B the left; moving both together moves X, moving them oppositely moves Y 04 — A/B drives
    Access hole The small round hole near each end of a vertical frame extrusion. The hex key goes through it to reach the screw of a blind joint 01.5
    ADXL345 The accelerometer built into the Nitehawk-SB V2, used by SHAPER_CALIBRATE to measure resonance 14.12
    Arachne PrusaSlicer's variable-width perimeter generator, kept on for every plate: it widens or narrows a bead to fill Voron's thin ribs instead of leaving a gap. The 0.40 mm width in the override table is Arachne's target, not a fixed bead print/00-slicer-setup — Overrides
    Anodising The hard oxide layer on the extrusions. It is an electrical insulator, which is why it gets scraped under the frame PE washer 10.16
    ASA The material this whole build is printed in, to Voron's p.4 spec, which is written for ABS: 0.2 mm layers, 0.4 mm extrusion width, 4 perimeters, 5 top/bottom, 40 % infill 00.23

    Source: Ch 04 · Ch 14 Step 14.12 · Ch 00 Step 00.23

    B¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    Babystepping Moving Z live, in 0.01 mm steps, while the first layer prints. Discarded on restart unless committed with Z_OFFSET_APPLY_ENDSTOP and then SAVE_CONFIG 13.42
    Backer (titanium extrusion backer) A titanium strip bolted to the face opposite a linear rail, to cancel the bimetallic bow a steel rail induces in an aluminium extrusion 05.16
    Batch BOM LDO's per-batch bill of materials, found by the serial on carton 1. Every batch tile still opens the Rev D page, so this kit inventories against LDO's separate Rev D+ page 00.2
    Bed mesh A grid of probed heights that Klipper applies as a Z correction during a print. Taken hot and after QGL, never before 12.34
    BHCS Button Head Cap Screw — domed head, mostly M5 in this build 00.25
    Blind joint The frame joint: a BHCS head slides into one extrusion's slot and threads into the tapped end of the mating extrusion, tightened through an access hole 00.28
    Boss The thick ring of plastic round a screw hole in a printed part. It is what the screw or insert grips, so a cracked or bulged one means a reprint 02.01
    BMG The dual-geared filament drive inside Clockwork 2.5 — the idler assembly you grease and the tension arm that loads it 08.9
    Cam (belt tensioner) A lever with an off-centre round end. Swinging it pushes the Z motor away from its drive shaft and tightens the short belt 02.33
    Brim A flat single-layer ring printed around a part's base to hold its corners down. Every part 30 mm or longer carries a 3 mm one, set 0.3 mm off the part so it snaps away print/00-slicer-setup — Orientation & brim
    Burr A rough raised edge left where metal was cut. It stops two parts sitting flat against each other 01.3
    Blanchard-ground Machined flat by a spinning grinder, which leaves a swirl pattern on the metal 03.3
    Blind hole A hole that stops partway into the part instead of going through 04.3
    Belt plane The height a belt runs at. The A and B belts run 10 mm apart so they never touch 05.8
    Back-EMF The voltage a stepper motor generates when its shaft is turned, by hand or otherwise. Spun fast while connected, it can kill the motor drivers 06.25
    Bearing stack Flanged bearings piled on one screw. They turn a belt without driving it 07.1
    Backlash The tiny free play left between two meshed gears 08.24
    Bridge PCB The small Nevermore board that joins both fans and the pigtail onto one 24 V lead 11.29
    Bootloader A small program that runs first at power-up and can load new firmware onto a board 12.14
    Bed-slinger A printer whose bed moves in Y. The Voron is not one 14.13

    Source: Ch 05 Step 05.16 · Ch 00 Steps 00.2, 00.25, 00.28 · Ch 08 Step 08.9

    C¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    CAN toolboard A toolboard that talks to the host over CAN bus on a two-wire umbilical. This build has none — the Nitehawk-SB V2 is a USB toolboard, so it appears as a second Klipper MCU on /dev/serial/by-id 10.54
    Carriage The small block that rolls along a linear rail on steel balls. It must never run off the end of the rail 02.05
    CAT rating A multimeter's IEC 61010 measurement category — how big a transient it can survive where you use it, not what voltage it can display 00a.3
    Checkpoint #1 LDO's mains gate, run with only the inlet, the WAGO bus and the PSU on the mains: meter checks on the unplugged machine, then one deliberate power-on. The SSR and bed are wired after it, dead. The bay does not close until it and Checkpoint 10 pass Ch 10 Section 2
    Clicky-Clack The magnetic-latch front door kit (LDO, blue frame) fitted instead of the stock Voron doors 11.44
    Clockwork 2.5 (CW2.5) The Voron extruder that mounts to the Stealthburner r2 — BMG drive, printed body with a push-in Bowden collet, its own insert pass. Successor to Clockwork 2 (CW2), with no anti-squish screw 08.3
    COB light strip The chamber lighting: two strips on printed mounts, joined by a splitter PCB, on the Leviathan's LED-Strip port 12.32
    Collet The push-in fitting on top of the Clockwork 2.5 that grips the PTFE tube; its clip stops the tube creeping. To free the tube, take the clip off, press the collet's ring down and pull the tube out 08.14
    CoreXY The motion system: two fixed motors drive two belts, stacked one above the other so they never cross (the Voron leaves out the crossing many CoreXY printers have, p.125); neither motor moves the bed or itself. One motor alone moves the head diagonally, so a diagonal move when you asked for X or Y means A and B are wrong 04 — A/B drives
    Counterbore A wider recess round a hole that a screw head sinks into, so the head sits below the surface 02.04
    Cross-threaded Said of a screw started at an angle, so its threads bind and cut a new, wrong thread 03.15
    Clearance hole A hole slightly wider than its screw, so the screw slides through without threading 04.4
    Cable bridge The blue arch on the right XY joint that holds the moving end of the toolhead drag chain clear of the belts; manual p.83 labels it XY Bridge 05.3
    Countersink A cone-shaped hole that lets a flat-head screw (FHCS) sit flush 05.17
    Captive nut A nut held in a printed pocket so that a screw can thread into it 07.26
    Conduit A printed channel stuck to the deck that carries the bay's wires, with snap-on lids 09.6
    Continuity The meter mode that beeps when its two probes are joined by a conducting path 10.5
    Crowsnest The MainsailOS webcam service that serves the camera stream 11.76
    Commented out Said of a config line turned off by a leading #, so that Klipper ignores it 12.24

    Source: Ch 10 Step 10.54 · Ch 00a Step 00a.3 · Ch 08 Step 08.3 · Ch 11 Step 11.44

    D¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    Deck panel The acrylic floor of the chamber, above the electronics bay. Its thickness (3 or 4 mm) is disputed between LDO's own documents — caliper it 02.12
    D-cut (flat) The flat side ground on a motor or drive shaft. A set screw presses on it so the pulley cannot slip round 02.17
    DFU STM32's built-in USB bootloader mode, entered by a request from a running Klipper or, as recovery, with the board's buttons. The way in when Katapult is missing or has been overwritten 12.16
    DIN rail The standard 35 mm steel mounting rail. This bay has two, running left to right across both bed extrusions, as manual p.29 and LDO both draw them. The rear one, under the SSR, gets its own PE lead at 10.82 09.5
    Drag chain The articulated plastic chain carrying cables to a moving part. Three here: X, Y and Z. Cables inside must stay loose 10.59
    Dry-fit Holding parts in place without screws to check that they fit before fastening them 02.29
    Drying Heating filament to drive out absorbed moisture. ASA needs it only if the spool has been open a while or shows bubbling print/00-slicer-setup — Drying
    DSI ribbon Display Serial Interface — the flat cable from the Raspberry Pi to the touchscreen. The Pi's own display port, not USB, and it latches at both ends 10 — Wiring
    Datum The flat reference surface that everything else is measured from 03.1
    Drive unit A rear corner block that holds bearing stacks and the A or B motor. Its pulley drives one belt 04.1
    De-racking Pushing the X extrusion hard against both drives to square a racked gantry, then tightening the XY joints 06b.12
    DHCP reservation A router setting that always gives a device the same network address 12.4

    Source: Ch 02 Step 02.12 · Ch 12 Step 12.16 · Ch 09 Step 09.5 · Ch 10 Step 10.59

    E¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    Elephant foot The first layer coming out wider than the rest, from squish plus heat. Corrected with the slicer's compensation, in 0.05 mm steps — first on the Core One+ (Gate A's first-layer-vs-mid-height check), again on the Voron print/00-slicer-setup — Overrides; 14.11
    E-RV The hotend code embossed on both Stealthburner printhead halves for E3D Revo Voron. Read it before you build — it decides which printhead folder you slice 08.1
    ESD ground The Rev D+ path that drains static from the extruder motor body through the toolboard, the umbilical and the USB adapter to the earthed frame. The V2 partial USB-adapter cover leaves the adapter's grounding point open for it 10.58
    Extrusion multiplier / flow The slicer's scaling of how much plastic is extruded. On the Voron (Ch 14) it is set by eye on the centre of a top surface, never from caliper readings, and is not a size lever: Voron parts need no compensation beyond a good multiplier tune (Ellis). The one exception is the Core One+ before B01: a Gate A cube or a Gate B 625-2RS pocket still off size with shrinkage compensation at 0 % and XY compensation at 0 moves the multiplier in 1 % steps. Never negative XY size compensation, on either printer Gate A; 14.19
    Endstop A switch or sensor that tells the printer an axis has reached its end 07.38
    Extrusion slot cover A flexible strip pressed into an empty extrusion slot to hide a cable 10.38

    Source: Ch 14 Steps 14.11, 14.19 · Ch 08 Step 08.1 · Ch 10 Step 10.58

    F¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    F695 Flanged bearing, 5 × 13 × 4 mm — A/B drives, front idlers, XY joints. Twenty in the kit 00.26
    625-2RS Plain bearing, 5 × 16 × 5 mm — Z drives only. Same 5 mm bore as an F695, so sorting by bore does not work 00.26
    Feeler gauge A set of thin metal leaves, each of a marked thickness. The thickest leaf that slides into a gap tells you how big the gap is 00.10
    Ferrule A crimped metal sleeve on a stranded wire end so a screw terminal clamps a solid tube instead of loose strands. The kit ships five loose VE0508, for a re-terminated hotend heater lead only; the mains leads arrive with fork lugs 00a.8
    Fork lug A U-shaped metal tag crimped on a wire end, clamped flat under a screw so no loose strand reaches it. Every PSU and SSR lead in the kit arrives with an insulated one already on 00a.8
    FFC Flat Flexible Cable — a thin ribbon with bare contacts at each end, held by a latch in each socket. Half-latched is the usual failure 10 — Wiring
    FHCS Flat Head Cap Screw — countersunk cone head, flat top. The titanium backers use them, and they cam out easily in a countersink 00.25
    Flex plate The removable spring-steel print surface that sits on a magnetic pad. On the Core One+ the sheet follows the material: satin for ASA, PLA and PETG V0, textured for other PETG (print sheet). The Voron's own plate is the kit's, fitted at 03.20 B00.3
    Flange The raised rim beside a pulley's teeth that keeps the belt on, or the rim of a flanged bearing 02.17
    Flip-and-pack LDO's rail-greasing method: carriage down, grease forced through a mounting hole until it oozes past the bearings. Needs the back of the rail, so it only works before installation 00.19
    Front idler A front corner block with one bearing pair that the belt turns around, plus a tension arm to tighten it 04.1
    Fillet A rounded inside corner on a printed part 09.16

    Source: Ch 00 Steps 00.19, 00.25, 00.26 · Ch 00a Step 00a.8

    G¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    G28 Klipper's home-all command. On this machine it needs a reachable safe_z_home position, which is deliberately left invalid until you measure it 13.27
    G32 The Voron macro that homes, runs QGL and re-homes. Re-run it after every SAVE_CONFIG, which restarts Klipper and loses homing 12.27
    Gate A / Gate B The two halves of the B00 print gate. Gate A — the cube alone, no kit needed — releases B02, B07 and the cosmetics B08–B10, B12 and B13. Gate B — a caliper across the printed 625-2RS pocket and seven real heat-set inserts now, the bearing press and the MGN12 rail row on kit day — releases B01 and B03–B06 print/00-slicer-setup — Calibration sequence
    GT1.5 / GT2 Belt tooth pitch in mm. Gen 2 swaps the Core One's 2 mm GT2 belts and pulleys for 1.5 mm GT1.5, which raises the tooth-engagement ripple frequency and shrinks VFA. Pulleys, steps/mm and firmware all change together print/00-slicer-setup — Gen 2 belt upgrade
    Gantry The X extrusion, its two XY joints and the X carriage, carried on the two Y axes. Everything above the deck that moves in XY 05 — Gantry
    Gear ratio (80:16) How many motor turns make one turn of the driven shaft. On each Z drive the motor turns 5 times per shaft turn, and Klipper's gear_ratio must say the same 02.27
    GFCI / RCD Residual-current device — cuts the supply when live and neutral currents differ, which is what happens when current leaves through a person 00a.5
    Galling Surface damage from forcing a tight part, for example a T-nut tearing up the metal of a slot 03.12
    Guidler The extruder's swinging arm. It carries the idler gear and presses it onto the drive gear 08.5
    GPIO header The Raspberry Pi's double row of pins. The HAT power adapter plugs onto it 09.23

    Source: Ch 13 Step 13.27 · Ch 12 Step 12.27 · Ch 00a Step 00a.5

    H¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    Hammerhead nut A T-nut that drops in from the face of the slot rather than rolling in. Used exclusively for panel mounting in this build 00.25
    Handedness (_a / _b) Which of two mirror-image versions a printed part is, like a left and a right glove. The file name ends _a or _b 02.01
    Heat-set insert A brass M3×5×4 sleeve melted into a printed boss to give it a real thread. 153 in the kit; a missed one can cost a gantry teardown 00.13
    Heat soak Holding the machine at temperature until the frame, not just the bed, has expanded. Bed: minutes. Frame: until PROBE_ACCURACY stops trending, about 10–20 minutes open (13.31) and 1½–2 hours closed for the Z-joint lock (14.6) 13.31
    HV stepper The Leviathan's high-voltage stepper ports (Vin 24-48V supply). The A and B motors live here; Z motors are on the standard ports 10.25
    Heater pad The silicone mains heater bonded under the build plate. It is factory-fitted on this kit 03.4
    Hub The collar on one side of a pulley that holds its set screws 04.24
    HeaterCore The Revo's heater and thermistor in one module, held on the heatsink by a spring 08.27
    Host The Raspberry Pi half of Klipper, which does the planning and the arithmetic 12.1
    Hotend fan The Stealthburner's bottom fan. It cools the heatsink above the nozzle whenever the hotend is hot 13.9
    Heightmap Range / Heightmap variance Mainsail's number, in its Heightmap panel, for how far the bed mesh strays from flat: the highest point minus the lowest. Voron's docs and older screenshots call it Variance. Read it, not the colours 14.22

    Source: Ch 00 Steps 00.13, 00.25 · Ch 13 Step 13.31 · Ch 10 Step 10.25

    I¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    IEC C13 / C14 The kettle-lead connector pair — C14 is the male inlet on the machine, C13 the socket on the cord. Ch 10 keeps the cord in another room between rituals 09.11
    Idle timeout [idle_timeout] timeout: 1800 in the LDO config: 30 minutes without a move turns the heaters off and releases every motor, gantry included. Raise it with SET_IDLE_TIMEOUT TIMEOUT=7200 before a soak; RESTART, FIRMWARE_RESTART and SAVE_CONFIG put it back to 1800 13.31
    Idler A toothed or plain pulley that redirects a belt without driving it. F695 stacks in the A/B path, 20T idlers at the XY joints, a Z idler at the top of each vertical 04.7
    Inductive probe The Omron sensor that detects the metal bed for QGL only. It never sets Z=0 — the nozzle probe does 07.35
    Input shaper Klipper's anti-ringing filter, fitted to the machine's measured resonance. Saving it does not set max_accel; that is a separate, mandatory edit. Measured at 14.13 12.35
    IPA Isopropyl alcohol — the degreaser used before grease, tape or a first layer. ≥ 90 % for the rail soak 00 — Before you start

    Source: Ch 09 Step 09.11 · Ch 07 Step 07.35 · Ch 14 Steps 14.13, 14.15

    J¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    JST-PH2.0 2.0 mm-pitch connector. On the Nitehawk-SB V2 this is PROBE, TH0, CT and XY ENDSTOP — the Rev D guide's XH2.5 text is wrong for this board 10.55
    JST-XH2.5 2.5 mm-pitch connector — visibly coarser. On the V2 it is the MOTOR port. The pitch difference is how you identify a V2 board 00.5
    JST-ZH1.5 1.5 mm-pitch, 5-pin — the V2's secondary USB port, the physical marker that the board is a V2 and the reason to leave umbilical slack 00.5
    Jumper A small plug that bridges a pair of header pins to select a setting 10.3

    Source: Ch 10 Step 10.55 · Ch 00 Step 00.5

    K¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    Katapult A CAN/USB bootloader that lets you reflash without touching the board. Ch 12 installs it where a board lacks it; whether LDO's factory Leviathan carries it, the board shows at 12.15. Building Klipper with the wrong bootloader offset erases it 12.15
    KIAUH Klipper Installation And Update Helper — an installer for the Klipper ecosystem, used here only to add KlipperScreen; MainsailOS already has the rest 12.8
    Klicky A dockable microswitch probe. Its parts are printed (B06-P2) and bagged on this build as the alternative probe, not fitted and no magnets pressed — the kit's config, wiring and cable are for the inductive probe B06.4; 08.54
    Klipper The firmware: a host process on the Raspberry Pi plus thin firmware on each MCU. Configuration lives in printer.cfg on the Pi, not on the boards 12 — Software
    klippy.log Klipper's full log, at ~/printer_data/logs/klippy.log. Attach it complete and unmodified to any question — a snippet is not useful 15 — Where to ask
    Keystone A snap-in socket module, here the RJ45 network socket, for a rectangular panel cut-out 10.51
    KlipperScreen The touchscreen front end for Klipper. It runs on the Pi beside Mainsail 12.9

    Source: Ch 12 Steps 12.8, 12.15 · Ch 08 Step 08.54 · Klipper — Contact

    L¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    LDO Motor Dynamics Lab — the kit vendor whose Build Notes, Wiring Guide, Printed Parts Guide and BOM overlay the official Voron manual everywhere they differ 00.2
    LDO Build Notes / Build FAQ LDO's page-indexed diff against the official manual — which pages to skip, which parts differ. The source of most Rev D+ / LDO callouts Build FAQ
    LDO Wiring Guide (Rev D) LDO's own electrical walkthrough, and the source of Checkpoint #1. Written for a V1 toolboard, so its rp2040 and XH2.5 statements are wrong for a Rev D+ Wiring guide
    Linear rail A hardened steel track that a carriage rolls along. The four Z rails guide the gantry's corners up and down 02.05
    Leviathan LDO's mainboard, replacing the manual's BTT Octopus. Carries the Pi on standoffs and supplies its 5 V — there is no 5 V PSU in this kit 09.24
    Live–dead–live Prove the meter on a known-live source, take the reading, prove the meter again. The defence against a dead meter reading "no voltage" 00a.4
    Loctite 243 Medium-strength threadlocker, used on every pulley set screw and the Z drive fasteners 00.9
    Live Z Nudging the nozzle height while the first layer prints, then saving the result 14.21

    Source: Ch 00 Step 00.2 · Ch 09 Step 09.24 · LDO Build FAQ · LDO wiring guide

    M¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    M112 / emergency stop Klipper's emergency stop. Typed alone on the console line, Mainsail sends it round the command queue and Klipper shuts down at once. FIRMWARE_RESTART brings it back 13.22
    Mainsail The web interface for Klipper (with Moonraker as its API layer). Every g-code command in Ch 13–14 is typed in its Console tab 12.5
    make menuconfig / bootloader offset Klipper's firmware-build menu. The bootloader offset is where the firmware starts in flash, just past Katapult: 32 KiB on the Leviathan's F446, 128 KiB on its H743, 8 KiB on the Nitehawk. The wrong one erases Katapult 12.14
    max_accel The printer's acceleration ceiling. The LDO config ships a placeholder 10000; input shaping tells you the real number and SAVE_CONFIG will not write it for you 14.15
    Machinist square A precise L-shaped steel ruler. Held into a corner, any light between its blade and the part shows the corner is not 90° 01.9
    MCU Microcontroller: a processor board that turns Klipper's commands into pin signals. Two here, the Leviathan ([mcu]) and the Nitehawk-SB V2 ([mcu nhk]) 12.11
    Meanwell LRS-200-24 The 24 V 8.8 A PSU. Its 115/230 V selector is set once in Ch 09 and re-checked at Checkpoint #1; getting it wrong destroys the unit on first switch-on 09.14
    MGN9 / MGN12 Linear rail sizes. This build has one MGN12H (X) and six MGN9H (2 × Y, 4 × Z), all shipped in a shipping oil, not grease, and all degreased and greased in Ch 00 00.17
    Moonraker The API layer between Klipper and Mainsail. It also takes the slicer's uploads and answers the object query that proves the 350 edits 12.5
    Magnetic pad A magnetised rubber sheet with a sticky back, stuck to the build plate. It holds the flex plate down 03.1

    P¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    PE (protective earth) The green/yellow conductor that makes the machine safe to touch. Six branches here: supply, WAGO bus, PSU, frame, the SSR's DIN rail, bed 00a.6
    PEI sheet The coated spring-steel print surface. On the Core One+: satin for ASA, PLA and PETG V0, textured for other PETG. On either machine a good first layer still shows individual lines, not a featureless gloss 13.42
    PID tune Klipper's automatic heater-control calibration. Run before QGL, because a drifting probe gives a QGL that reports success and is wrong 13.29
    PIF (Print It Forward) The Voron programme that sells printed part sets. Explicitly not used on this build — every part is printed here 00.23
    Precision spacer The brass M5 1 mm spacer the kit supplies wherever the manual says M5 shim. A controlled thickness, not a washer — 46 in the kit 00.26
    Pressure advance Klipper's compensation for filament pressure lag in the nozzle, tuned from a printed pattern. Shifts when input shaping is switched on, so it comes after 14.17
    PRINT_START The macro the slicer calls at the top of every print, with the bed, hotend and chamber targets: home, heat, soak, level, mesh, then bring the hotend up. This manual replaces LDO's three-line stock one 12.36
    PROBE_ACCURACY Klipper's repeatability test for the probe. σ < 0.003 mm hot, with no trend, is the gate on QGL and everything downstream 13.32
    PTFE Polytetrafluoroethylene, the slippery white plastic. Here it is the tube that guides filament from the extruder down into the hotend, cut to stand 11 mm above the tool cartridge 08.32
    Purge line The line of filament the printer lays along the front edge before every print to prime the nozzle. On a colour change it is where you look for the old colour still streaking B02.1
    Pulley (16T / 20T / 80T) GT2 toothed pulleys. 16T on the Z motors, 20T everywhere else, 80T on the Z drive shafts — 16T and 20T look nearly identical and are not interchangeable 00.26
    Pinion The small gear on the motor shaft that turns the larger 50-tooth gear 08.13
    Plenum The printed air box of the Nevermore. It holds the two blowers and the carbon cartridge 11.26
    Pigtail A connector with a short tail of wire, cut from one lead to be soldered onto another 11.29
    Panel clip A printed clip that screws to the frame and holds a panel edge. The 4 mm or 6 mm in its name is the panel plus foam that it grips 11.53
    printer.cfg The one text file that tells Klipper what the machine is: pins, size, heaters, probe, fans and macros 12.21
    Part-cooling fan The Stealthburner's top 5015 fan. It blows on fresh plastic, on M106 or the slicer's command only 13.10
    Physical printer PrusaSlicer's saved link to a networked printer, used to upload G-code 13.41
    Purge bucket The optional scrubber's cup, hung on magnets under the brush bracket, that catches ooze and strings 13.49
    Pull-up resistor The fixed resistor on the board that a thermistor is read against. Klipper's pullup_resistor must match it: 2200 on the hotend's TH0 14.1

    R¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    Racking The gantry's X extrusion sitting out of square to the Y axes — a parallelogram instead of a rectangle. QGL passes happily on a racked gantry and the parts print skewed 05.44
    RESTART / FIRMWARE_RESTART RESTART reloads printer.cfg on the Pi; FIRMWARE_RESTART also resets both MCUs and is the way back from a shutdown. Neither is a stop: both wait behind a running move, so the stop is M112 13.22
    Rev D+ This kit: LDO Rev D plus the Nitehawk-SB V2. Exactly one new STL (usb_adapter_mount_partial_cover); the BOM drops the SD card and adds two ground wires and 20 extrusion blind-joint seals. LDO publishes no Rev D+ guide 00.5
    Revo Voron / Revo HF One hotend, two names: the E3D Revo Voron heatsink (printhead code E-RV, STL folder revo_voron) fitted with the HF high-flow nozzle — quick-change, screwed in by hand, which is why the strain relief has to be bent clear first. Verify against the kit's hotend box 08.27; printhead files at B06.2
    Roll-in T-nut A nut that drops into an extrusion slot and rotates to lock, so it can be added after assembly. Used everywhere except panel mounting 00.25
    Runout (sensor) The Core One+'s filament sensor: when the spool ends mid-plate the printer pauses, you load the next spool, it resumes with a seam at that layer — fine on a cosmetic part, never planned on a bearing-seat plate print/README — Spool ledger
    Rotation distance The millimetres of filament (or belt) moved per turn; on this extruder per turn of the drive gear, because its config sets a gear_ratio. Checked by extruding a measured 100 mm — not the lever for an oversize printed part. Refined at 14.7 13.40
    Rail guide A printed U-shaped clip that holds a rail centred on its extrusion while you start the screws 05.11
    Rail stopper A rubber stop near a rail end that keeps the carriage from running off 05.15
    Raspberry Pi The small computer that runs Klipper and commands the Leviathan 09.22
    Resonance The frequency a machine shakes at after a sharp move. Input shaping cancels it 14.14

    S¶

    Term What it is First matters at
    SAVE_CONFIG Klipper's command to write calibrated values into an auto-generated block at the end of printer.cfg. It restarts Klipper, so homing is lost and every motor releases each time 13.29
    "Second hole in" LDO's rule for the Y rails, which this manual applies to the Z rails too: skip a rail's end holes and start at the second hole from each end, because later T-nuts must pass the Y rail ends. The X rail is the exception: p.101 screws both of its end holes 00.22
    Seam Where each perimeter loop starts and stops, leaving a faint vertical line. Set to Nearest on the ASA plates, so it lands on a corner; smooth curves get a scarf joint instead print/00-slicer-setup — Overrides
    Set screw (grub screw) A headless screw that clamps a pulley to a shaft. One of the pair goes on the shaft's machined flat, and both get threadlocker 00.26
    SET_STEPPER_ENABLE Switches one motor on or off, for example SET_STEPPER_ENABLE STEPPER=stepper_x ENABLE=0. It frees X and Y while Z keeps holding the gantry. M84 releases all seven, so it is used only with a hand under the gantry 13.17
    SHCS Socket Head Cap Screw — straight cylindrical head. The most common fastener in the machine: 283 M3×8 alone 00.25
    Shim A thin DIN 988 spacer the official manual calls for at M5 locations. Your kit replaces every one with a brass precision spacer 00.26
    Skirt (machine) The ring of printed segments around the base that hides and closes the electronics bay, and carries the touchscreen and bay-fan modules 11.1
    Skirt (slicer) A loop of filament a slicer can draw around the plate before the parts start, to prime the nozzle. This build prints without one (Alex 2026-10-28): the start G-code's purge line primes instead. Not the machine's skirt print/00-slicer-setup — Overrides; B00.4
    Snug Just closed: the parts touch and stay put, but a mallet tap can still shift them. Final tightening comes later, when a step says so 01.7
    Snubber The RC network inside the SSR. It leaks a small current even with no input, which is why the SSR is only ever wired dead 00a.7
    Squish How hard the first layer is pressed into the sheet, set live during a print and committed with Z_OFFSET_APPLY_ENDSTOP. Moves again when extrusion multiplier changes 13.42
    SSH Secure Shell: the terminal connection from the laptop to the Pi, ssh <youruser>@voron.local, used for every command outside Mainsail 12.5
    SSR (solid-state relay) A semiconductor switch with no moving contacts, used here to switch mains to the bed. One that reads short when unpowered is dead and the bed would be permanently live 09.17
    Stealthburner The Voron toolhead body — printhead halves, fan ducts, LED logo, and the accent-coloured main body from batch B02 08.62
    STEPPER_BUZZ Klipper's per-motor test: 1 mm forward and back. Proves the right motor is on the right port and turning the right way, before anything homes 13.13
    Stepper motor A motor that turns in small exact steps. The A and B motors move 0.9° per step 04.1
    S-wrap A belt path that snakes round a bearing, the toothed motor pulley and a second bearing, so more teeth grip 07.3
    Stop screw A screw set to a height as a spacer, not to hold parts together 07.7
    Silkscreen The lettering printed on a circuit board 08.33
    Solder jumper Three pads on a circuit board. Joining the middle pad to one side with solder picks an option, here 24 V or 5 V 08.45
    Service loop Deliberate slack in a cable so that it never pulls tight at the ends of travel 08.63
    Strain relief A clamp or tie point that takes any pull on a cable so that the pull never reaches the connector or terminal. On the Revo HeaterCore it is the stainless sleeve where the wires leave the module, bent clear at Step 08.29 09.35 · 08.29
    Spade terminal A flat metal tab that a crimped push-on connector slides onto 09.11
    Self-tapping screw A screw that cuts its own thread in plastic. Driven in and out repeatedly, it strips the hole 09.20
    Splicer PCB A small board with no electronics on it that joins several plugs onto one lead 09.34
    Split bushing A rolled metal sleeve pressed into a printed hinge as its bearing surface 11.47
    Serial ID A board's USB path under /dev/serial/by-id/, named by chip family and a unique chip id. It stays the same across reboots 12.12
    Standard deviation The typical scatter of repeated readings, written σ. PROBE_ACCURACY passes at σ below 0.003 mm 13.28
    Spectrum analyser A phone app that shows the pitches in a sound. The lowest peak of a plucked belt is its tension reading 14.4
    Span The measured length of belt that you pluck. A frequency means nothing without it 07.30

    Source: Ch 00 Steps 00.22, 00.25, 00.26 · Ch 08 Step 08.62 · Ch 13 Steps 13.13, 13.42
