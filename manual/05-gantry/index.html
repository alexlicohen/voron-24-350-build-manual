    Chapter 05 — Gantry: X and Y axes, XY joints, X carriage, titanium backers¶

    One page per step

    Read this chapter one step per page — big pictures, prev/next, and the same tick marks. This page is the whole chapter in one scroll.

    You build the whole gantry on the bench: the rear E extrusion with both drives, both Y axes with their MGN9 rails, both XY joints, the X extrusion with its MGN12 rail, and the titanium extrusion backers. The chapter ends with the X axis riding on the Y carriages, and it unlocks Ch 06 (gantry install, Z belts, squaring).

    What you're building in this chapter: The gantry is the whole moving upper half of the printer: a rectangle of aluminium extrusion that later hangs on four belts and slides up and down, with the toolhead riding inside it. Its back edge is the E extrusion, the rear cross-beam that carries the A and B motors you built in Ch 04. Voron calls it the rear gantry extrusion (p.122). The manual's "XY Bridge" label on p.83 means something else: the blue cable bridge on the right XY joint. Two side extrusions, the Y axes, run forward from the E extrusion. Each has a linear rail along one face and a front idler block at its front end that turns its belt back. Spanning the two sides is the X beam, capped at each end by an XY joint: the printed blocks that ride on the Y carriages and turn each belt from its Y run to its X run. Screwed to the face opposite every rail are the titanium backers, flat strips that stop a steel rail bowing its aluminium extrusion as the chamber heats. Nothing in this chapter gets belts or squaring: both belong to Ch 06 and Ch 07, and doing either now would undo work done here.

    Time: 5.0–7.0 h hands-on, first build, two people (survey §5.1 P05 / §7.2).

    Sessions: 12 × ~30 min. Each one ends at a safe place to stop, and each Pause: line carries its own minutes, a first-build estimate.

    Prerequisites:

    • Ch 04 is done: the A drive, the B drive and both front idler assemblies are built and checked (manual p.62–81). This chapter uses them whole.
    • Ch 00 is done: all seven rails are cleaned and packed with grease before they go on an extrusion (LDO rail grease guide), and the rail guides Tools/MGN9_rail_guide_x2.stl and Tools/MGN12_rail_guide_x2.stl are printed (batch B00). The rails still wear their Ch 00 end bands (Step 00.17); they come off at Steps 05.11 and 05.33.
    • Printed parts: batch B04 (XY joints and X carriage, plate B04-P1, 8.8 h, black) and batch B02, blue plates P1, P2 and P4: the endstop pod is on P1, the 2-hole cable bridge on P2 and the raised cable bridge on P4, and the four upper Z-belt clips are one each on P1 and P2 and two on P4 (print plan §B04). B04 needs Gate B (Step B00.7).
    • The titanium backer set (Fabreeko/LDO, 350 size) is unpacked and counted, and 61 M3 roll-in T-nuts are counted out from the kit bag: 32 for the rails and extrusion ends, 29 for the backers. The backer set ships none.
    • Extrusions: the C ×2, D ×1 and E ×1 you bagged in Ch 01.

    Tools

    • Hex drivers 2, 2.5, 3 and 4 mm. A ball-end 2.5 mm driver, whose rounded tip works at an angle, helps at the XY joints.
    • T10 Torx driver, strongly preferred for the backers' M3 FHCS: a hex key slips out of a countersunk screw head easily (backers README).
    • The printed rail guides MGN9_rail_guide_x2 and MGN12_rail_guide_x2, which hold each rail centred on its extrusion.
    • A 300 mm steel rule and a caliper, to make the rail overhang equal at both ends.
    • Masking tape to hold the carriages on their rails, and the kit's rubber rail stoppers.
    • A 2.5 mm flat screwdriver for the cable-chain latches, if you dry-fit the chain end.

    Read first

    • The titanium backers go on in this chapter, not after it. Fitting them once the gantry is built means taking it apart. They go on the face opposite the rail: the top of the Y extrusions and the rear of the X extrusion.
    • Do not tension the A/B belts and do not square the gantry here. Both happen in Ch 06 and Ch 07, and the official squaring starts by releasing belt tension, so doing either now would just be undone. Several joints are left slightly loose on purpose.
    • Every T-nut goes in before the drive and idler blocks cap the extrusion ends. The kit's T-nuts are a tight fit, so test-fit them first or load them early.
    • A carriage slides off its rail easily, and a dropped one is ruined. Tape or stop every carriage the moment its rail is on an extrusion. The rails must already be greased (Ch 00); if they are not, stop and do that first.
    • Which way up. Until Step 05.41 the gantry is built the way it runs: motors hanging down below the E extrusion, Y rails underneath. Then it turns upside down (motors up, rails up) so the X axis can be lowered onto the Y carriages, and it stays that way until Ch 06 turns it back (Step 06.11). Every step that turns it over says so.

    Notes behind "Read first"

    • The titanium backers go on during this chapter, not after it. Fitting them once the gantry is assembled or installed means a teardown (survey §5.2 W2). They mount on the face opposite the rail — top of the Y extrusions, rear of the X extrusion. "If you have a single MGN12 but decide to put a backer on top, you will be actively contributing to the problem of bimetallic expansion!" src
    • Do not tension the A/B belts and do not square the gantry here. Both are Ch 06/07, and the official squaring procedure begins by releasing A/B tension — tension it now and you undo it (survey §4.4 #2, §5.2 W1). Several joints in this chapter are deliberately left "slightly loose".
    • Every T-nut this chapter needs must go in before the drive and idler frames cap the extrusion ends. LDO: "Due to the tight tolerances of the extrusions and roll-in t-nuts it is advisable to either test fit before assembly … or to pre-load the t-nuts into the extrusions." src
    • Short a part? This chapter needs exact counts: 4 F695, 4 spacers, 2 20T idlers, the backer screws, and 61 M3 roll-in T-nuts (the build uses 134 of the kit's 135). Recount against your Ch 00 inventory sheet, then contact Fabreeko support; do not borrow from another chapter's bag.
    • Rails ship dry. If Ch 00's clean-and-pack pass has not been done, stop and do it — the flip-and-pack method needs access to the back of the rail (survey §4.4 #5).
    • Carriages slide off rails and are ruined by a drop (manual p.88, p.101). Tape or stopper every carriage the moment its rail is on an extrusion.
    • Orientation. Through p.105 the gantry is built the way it runs — motors hanging down below the E extrusion, Y rails underneath (p.85, p.116). p.106 turns it upside down (motors up, rails up) so the X axis can be lowered onto the Y carriages; it stays that way to the end of this chapter and is turned back in Ch 06 (Step 06.11). Every step that turns it over says so.

    Step 05.1 — Clear the bench and confirm the sub-assemblies¶

    What you're looking at: This step gets the bench ready. The gantry is the moving frame above the bed that carries the toolhead: a rectangle of extrusions whose X beam slides along two side rails. The picture shows it finished, so you can see where the four Ch 04 sub-assemblies land at the corners.

    Parts:

    • reused: A drive assembly
    • reused: B drive assembly
    • reused: A idler assembly, front right
    • reused: B idler assembly, front left
    • staged: C extrusion ×2
    • staged: D extrusion ×1
    • staged: E extrusion ×1

    Do:

    1. Clear a bench at least 700 × 700 mm.
    2. Lay the four Ch 04 sub-assemblies out, drives at the top and idlers at the bottom.
    3. Keep the A drive on the right and the B drive on the left.

    Check

    All four Ch 04 assemblies match their CHECK YOUR WORK pages (p.68 and p.72 for the idlers, p.76 and p.80 for the drives), and all four spin freely.

    Source: Voron manual p.82 · Video: Part 4 @0:06:30

    Step 05.2 — Name the gantry parts¶

    What you're looking at: The same rectangle, now labelled. A and B are the two CoreXY motors; neither one on its own "is" an axis. The E extrusion across the back holds the drives apart, the XY joints cap the ends of the X beam, and the front idlers turn each belt back.

    Parts: none.

    Do:

    1. Learn the names: B Drive rear left, A Drive rear right, XY Bridge (the blue cable bridge on the right joint), both XY Joints, X Linear Rail, B Idler front left, A Idler front right.
    2. Check the A and B labels you put on the Ch 04 parts.

    Check

    You can point at the A drive, the B idler and the right XY joint without hesitating.

    Helper: Points to each gantry part as you name it and repeats the name back.

    Source: Voron manual p.83

    Step 05.3 — Pick the cable-chain bridge¶

    What you're looking at: The cable bridge is the blue arch on the right XY joint that lifts the toolhead's drag chain (the hinged track carrying its cables) clear of the belts. It comes in two printed versions: three holes or two. LDO's printed-parts guide picks the 2-hole configuration for this kit's chains, so every chain part here is the 2-hole version. src

    Parts:

    • [a]_xy_joint_cable_bridge_2hole.stl ×1 (blue)

    Do:

    1. Find the 2-hole bridge from plate B02-P2 and its raised alternate from B02-P4; you printed no 3-hole part.
    2. Confirm the bridge has two holes on the raised pad, and set it beside the raised alternate.

    Check

    One 2-hole bridge and one raised alternate are on the bench, and no 3-hole part exists in this build.

    The raised alternate, XY_cable_chain_bridge-Igus-3mm_backer.stl, has the same 2-hole pattern but stands 3 mm taller, to match the titanium Y backer under the chain's fixed end. Step 05.35 picks it. src

    Source: Voron manual p.84 · LDO printed-parts guide (Rev D) · Ti extrusion-backers README (tanaes) · Video: Part 3 @1:43:24

    Step 05.4 — Test-fit T-nuts and stage the fasteners¶

    What you're looking at: A roll-in T-nut is a small nut that drops into an extrusion's slot and turns a quarter turn to lock, and almost every screw in this chapter pulls against one. The renders show the four extrusions, E, both C and D, with every T-nut already in its slot.

    Parts:

    • staged: M5 roll-in T-nut ×26
    • staged: M3 roll-in T-nut ×61

    Do:

    1. Roll one M5 and one M3 T-nut through each face of all four extrusions.
    2. Mark any face that binds with tape.
    3. Count the fasteners out into labelled trays, following the chapter's Hardware table.

    Check

    Every T-nut you will use rolls in without force, and every stiff face is marked.

    Helper: Counts the T-nuts and bolts into their piles and checks them against the list.

    Caution

    Never force a T-nut. Extrusions and roll-in T-nuts fit tightly in this kit, and a nut forced along a slot scrapes and tears the aluminium, which cannot be undone. If one will not turn, try another face. src

    The CAD shows the 250-size extrusions (E 240, C 350, D 330 mm); yours are the 350 set. What carries over is which faces take nuts, and how many.

    Source: LDO Build Notes § Voron 2.4 build FAQ · Voron manual p.85 · Video: Part 4 @0:36:43 · CAD: Voron 2.4r2 STEP @ de7e89d

    Good stopping point · ~25 min since the last one

    bench cleared, sub-assemblies laid out left and right, T-nuts test-rolled and counted into trays. Nothing is assembled yet. Cover the trays so nothing gets swept off.

    Step 05.5 — Load the E extrusion (rear beam)¶

    What you're looking at: The E extrusion is the rear beam of the gantry: it holds the two drive blocks a fixed distance apart, and so sets the gantry's width. It is not the "XY Bridge": on p.83 that label means the cable bridge. The eight M5 T-nuts are what those blocks screw down onto.

    Parts:

    • E extrusion ×1
    • M5 T-nut ×8

    Do:

    1. Slide eight M5 T-nuts into the E extrusion: at each end, two in the top slot and two in the bottom slot. None go in the side slots.
    2. Push them 10–15 mm in from the ends, under the drive frame's screw holes.

    Check

    Each end holds 4 nuts, 2 top and 2 bottom, threaded holes facing outward and none jammed.

    Source: Voron manual p.85

    Step 05.6 — Slide the A drive onto the E extrusion¶

    What you're looking at: The A drive is the right-hand assembly from Ch 04: a stepper motor with its 20-tooth pulley, wrapped in a printed frame that carries the belt bearing posts. It slides over the end of the E extrusion like a sleeve.

    Parts:

    • reused: A drive assembly
    • reused: E extrusion with its eight T-nuts

    Do:

    1. Push the A drive onto one end of the E extrusion, motor pointing down. It stays this way up until the flip at Step 05.41.
    2. Seat it until the printed part is flush (level) with the extrusion end.

    Check

    The plastic is flush with the aluminium end face, and you can see the four M5 T-nuts through the drive frame's screw holes.

    Source: Voron manual p.85

    Step 05.7 — Bolt the A drive to the rear beam¶

    What you're looking at: Four M5×10 BHCS (button-head screws) pass through the drive frame's flange into the T-nuts underneath. They make the drive block and the E extrusion one rigid rear corner. A screw that spins freely means its T-nut never turned to lock, and the corner is holding on three.

    Parts:

    • M5×10 BHCS ×4

    Do:

    1. Drive four M5×10 BHCS, two into the upper slot and two into the lower, to snug, not torqued.
    2. If a screw spins without pulling, its T-nut has not turned: back the screw out and reseat the nut.

    Check

    All four screws bite, and the drive frame does not rock on the extrusion.

    Source: Voron manual p.86

    Step 05.8 — Slide the B drive onto the other end¶

    What you're looking at: The B drive is the left-hand partner, not an identical copy. Its motor pulley sits at 6.5 mm where the A drive's sits at 16.5 mm, which puts the two belts at different heights, called belt planes, so they can cross without touching.

    Parts:

    • reused: B drive assembly

    Do:

    1. Push the B drive onto the free end of the E extrusion, motor down, flush with the end face.
    2. Look at the motor cable exits: they must point towards each other. If they point apart, the drives are swapped end for end.

    Check

    Both motor cable exits face inward, and both printed parts are flush.

    Source: Voron manual p.86

    Step 05.9 — Bolt the B drive and check the rear beam¶

    What you're looking at: With both drives screwed on, the E extrusion and drives become a single rear beam: the fixed back edge of the gantry, and the reference everything else is built to. The lower drawing in the picture is your alignment check.

    Parts:

    • M5×10 BHCS ×4

    Do:

    1. Fit the last four M5×10 BHCS, snug.
    2. Compare with the lower drawing: the drive frames are parallel, and the two pulleys sit one belt plane apart, one high and one low, as drawn.

    Check

    The drives are parallel with no twist along the E extrusion, the pulleys are one plane apart, and all eight M5×10 are in and snug. Nothing here is torqued yet.

    The lower drawing is seen from behind, so A is on the left: A's pulley sits high, B's low. Never move a pulley to make them match: the 10 mm step keeps the belts apart.

    Source: Voron manual p.87

    Good stopping point · ~30 min since the last one

    E extrusion screwed to both drive units, all eight M5×10 snug, none torqued. Lay the assembly flat, motors down. Do not start a Y rail: a rail must be centred and screwed down in one sitting.

    Step 05.10 — Load M3 T-nuts for the first Y rail¶

    What you're looking at: The two C extrusions are the Y axes, each with an MGN9 linear rail on one face. Its M3 T-nuts anchor every rail screw; once the rail is down, no more can go in.

    Parts:

    • C extrusion ×1
    • M3 T-nut ×10

    Do:

    1. Count the holes on rail LDO-SLR9H-400Z0: LDO's drawing spaces them 20 mm apart, so expect 20. src
    2. Mark the second hole from each end, as LDO asks, then every second hole between: 2, 4, 6 … 18 and 19 on 20 holes. Two marks side by side at one end is expected.

    Common mistakes¶

    • Fitting the backers after the gantry is built. The idler and drive frames cap their T-nut slots, and the backer face is buried once the gantry is in the frame. Fixing it is a teardown of about 3 h (survey §5.2 W2).
    • Putting the X backer on top of the X extrusion. With a single MGN12 on the front face, a top backer adds to the bimetallic bow (two metals expanding at different rates) instead of cancelling it, and it blocks the top slot the XY joints and cable bridge screw into.
    • Using the Y rails' end holes. The manual forbids it only on 300 mm builds; LDO's note forbids it on the Y rails of every build and uses the second hole from each end. The X rail is different: p.101 screws both of its end holes. Caught late, the rail comes off and goes back on.
    • Swapping the two Y backers. Only one has the cable-chain pilot holes, and the Y chain's fixed end needs them at the rear of the right Y. Found in Ch 10, it means unscrewing a backer that the drive and idler frames already cap.
    • Forgetting the reserved T-nuts from p.90. Nothing in this chapter uses them, so they are easy to skip, and then the screws of Ch 06's Z bearing blocks have nowhere to go. Count per slot before the idlers go on: 2 M5 in the top slot, 1 M5 and 1 M3 in the rail slot.
    • One spacer in an XY joint stack, or a flange facing inward. Either the pair drops 1 mm and its inner rings are never clamped, or there is no belt channel at all. It shows up in Ch 07 as belt rub, and both joints come apart again (3 M5×40 and the idler each). The fix: spacer, flange down, flange up, spacer, read from the side before you close the joint (p.97, p.99).
    • Torquing the XY joints and "squaring" on the bench. The joints are meant to stay slightly loose, and the gantry is squared in the frame with the belts slack. Tightening now, or tensioning belts now, costs a full de-tension in Ch 07 (survey §5.2 W1).
    • Leaving the blue upper Z-belt clips off the idlers and drives. Nothing in this chapter loads them, but Ch 06 clamps every Z belt's second end under them (p.117, p.120). Fitting them later means backing out M5×16 screws on a gantry that is already in the frame.
    • Over-tightening the 20T idler screws. They thread straight into plastic and only locate the idler. Crushed pockets show up later as belt noise you will blame on tension.
