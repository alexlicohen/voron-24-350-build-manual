# F5 — Read-through review: Ch 08 (toolhead) and Ch 09 (electronics bay)

Method: both chapters read twice (first-time, second-time), every referenced image opened (SB pp.11–32/37–56/64–67, manual pp.28–29/129/144/146–172, all mirrored LDO/Nitehawk images, the three SVG diagrams, the 09.26 CAD renders), cross-checked against Ch 02/05/07/10/11 text. Nitehawk-SB-V2 README fetched to confirm the PH2.0/keyed-header claims. LDO wiki pages are JS-only and could not be fetched; claims that rest on them are marked (verify).

### F1 · BLOCKER · 09-electronics-bay.md:09.11
Persona: first-time
Quote: "Confirm the module's factory wiring against LDO's inlet layout diagram: wired correctly, the live conductor is fused and the switch lights when on."
Problem: The only way a first-timer can see "the switch lights when on" is to plug the cord in. The module's spade terminals are bare and exposed in an open bay at this point, and the daughter is in the room. Nothing else in the chapter says how to "confirm the factory wiring" without energising it. This is the one crack in the "nothing is wired, nothing is plugged in" promise; the rest of Ch 09 keeps it.
Fix: Replace the ⚠ sentence with: "Compare by eye only: the back of the module has three spades and a short internal link from the fuse holder to the switch — match them to LDO's `inlet_layout.png` (L fused, N direct, PE to the earth pin). Nothing is plugged in and nothing lights up in this chapter; the lit rocker is Ch 10's Checkpoint #1 reward." Caption the step image: "LDO's photo shows the inlet after Ch 10 has wired it — yours has bare spades."

### F2 · MAJOR · 08-toolhead.md:08.11 (also 08.5, 08.20, hardware table)
Persona: first-time
Quote: "Slide the spring then the washer onto the thumbscrew shaft in that order, and thread the assembly into the guidler arm's threaded boss."
Problem: SB p.18 (zoomed) shows the thumbscrew passing *through* the guidler's top boss and into a separate hooked block sitting on top of the arm — `[a]_latch_shuttle`, which the chapter prints (B02), lists in its parts table, and never fits. p.15 shows an insert going into that same small hooked block with the Voron heart (end face), plus the latch cross-section and the guidler — three inserts, not the two counted in 08.5, so the chapter's total is 16, not 15. A first-timer finishes 08.11 with a thumbscrew threading into a through-hole, an orange part left over, and (if the shuttle's insert is real) a heat-set job on a part that is about to be trapped in the guidler. 08.20 then says the latch "holds the guidler arm closed" — it actually hooks the shuttle.
Fix: 08.5 Parts: add `[a]_latch_shuttle` ×1, insert ×1 — "insert enters the shuttle's end face; the p.15 'flush' callout is on the latch cross-section (verify on bench which part it belongs to)". 08.11 Do: "Seat the latch shuttle on top of the guidler boss with its hook facing the latch side (p.18, right-hand drawing). Pass the thumbscrew — spring, then washer, under the head — through the boss and thread it into the shuttle's insert. Check: the shuttle is captive and can be drawn against the spring; the latch (08.20) hooks over it." Hardware table: inserts 16; M3×16 row "joins the guidler halves", not "guidler pivot".

### F3 · MAJOR · 08-toolhead.md:08.1 (also 08.29)
Persona: first-time
Quote: "Confirm the rear half is the `_rear_cw2` variant: it has the corner opening for Clockwork 2 wire routing that the CW1 variant lacks." / Check: "the rear half's wire-routing corner is open."
Problem: SB p.37 says the opposite: "We also provide tool cartridge parts for ClockWork1, and other extruders … They are identical except for an opening in the highlighted corner to help with wire routing for those designs." The CW2 part is the one *without* the opening. The Check fails on the correctly printed part, and the natural response is to reprint the wrong (cw1) file. 08.29 repeats the error ("the wires leave through the CW2 corner opening").
Fix: 08.1: "Confirm the rear half is the `_rear_cw2` variant: its corner is solid. A rear half with a notch cut out of that corner is the CW1/other-extruder variant — wrong for this build (SB p.37)." Check: "`E-RV` on both halves; the highlighted corner is closed." 08.29: delete "through the CW2 corner opening"; route the wires as p.40 draws them.

### F4 · MAJOR · 08-toolhead.md:08.35
Persona: first-time
Quote: "Fit the **opaque black mask** behind and the **clear PETG diffuser** in front — the mask blocks light bleed so the logo reads crisply, the diffuser spreads it."
Problem: SB p.47 (zoomed): the diffuser's three prongs point *into* the mask's three windows, and the assembled view shows the prongs flush in the mask's outer face. The mask is the outermost layer; the diffuser's base sits behind it against the LED carrier. Reversed as written, the prongs face the LED and nothing fits; a first-timer will force it. The step's Check ("mask is the opaque part, diffuser the translucent one") does not catch the front/behind error.
Fix: "Order from the outside in: mask (opaque, three windows) → diffuser (three prongs forward through the windows, flat base behind) → LED carrier with the LED. Check: three translucent bars flush in the black face and no light path around them."

### F5 · MAJOR · 08-toolhead.md:08.28 (order with 08.29)
Persona: first-time
Quote: "Then carefully bend the HeaterCore's stainless strain relief so it clears the printed part."
Problem: At 08.28 the hotend is not in a printed part — 08.29 seats it. SB p.40 draws the hotend already in the rear half while the wires are routed and the relief bent. Bending blind means re-bending; the relief is the crimped wire exit of a 60 W HeaterCore, and it fatigues.
Fix: Swap 08.28 and 08.29 (seat in the rear half first, then route wires and bend the relief with the part in hand, p.40 as the image). Check: "with the rear half in your hand the relief clears its wall and the nozzle still turns the last part-turn by hand."

### F6 · MAJOR · 08-toolhead.md:08.53 (with 08.43)
Persona: first-time
Quote: "Run the short grounding cable from the toolboard's grounding point to the extruder motor body, bending the connector at an angle on the motor end so it clears the cable-chain anchor."
Problem: No fastener is named at either end, and the step comes 10 steps after the board was bolted down. LDO's `toolboard_ground_routing.jpg` (the step's own image) shows the board-end lug under the screw in the board's central plated hole — one of the two M3×8 tightened at 08.43 — and the motor-end lug on a short screw in one of the NEMA14's two unused face holes. A first-timer does not know a ring lug goes under an existing screw, or that a spare motor hole exists.
Fix: At 08.43: "put the supplied short ground cable's ring lug under the centre M3×8 as the board goes on." 08.53 becomes the motor end only: "short screw (supplied — verify) into the free motor face hole nearest the chain anchor; bend the lug so it clears the anchor (photo, right)." Add both screws to Parts and the hardware table.

### F7 · MAJOR · 08-toolhead.md:08.63
Persona: first-time
Quote: "zip-tie it at the anchor and at the chain's first link. Leave a visible **service loop** of slack between the anchor and the chain"

### F9 · MAJOR · 09-electronics-bay.md:09.5 (⚠, also 09.5 WYLA, Common mistakes #1)
Persona: first-time
Quote: "the manual runs the rails **front-to-back**, each rail bolted twice into the same extrusion. LDO runs them **left-to-right**"
Problem: Manual p.28 (zoomed) shows the two bed extrusions running front-to-back with two M5 T-nuts each; p.29 shows both DIN rails running left-to-right across them, one M5×10 into each extrusion — exactly LDO's layout; p.149/169/170/171 insets agree. The deviation is invented, repeated as a Common Mistake, and a first-timer comparing the ⚠ against p.29 loses time working out which drawing to believe. LDO's "These DIN rails run from left to right" is a description, not a correction.
Fix: Delete the ⚠ and Common-mistake #1; keep "left-to-right, one T-nut per bed extrusion, exactly as p.29 draws"; keep the second ⚠ about cut length.

### F10 · MAJOR · 09-electronics-bay.md:09.3 (convention used in 09.2, 09.12–09.13, 09.16, 09.18, 09.24, 09.26)
Persona: first-time
Quote: "turn the printer onto its top with two people" / 09.2: "Rear rail: Omron SSR at the left, Meanwell LRS-200-24 to its right"
Problem: Left/right is used ~15 times with the printer inverted and never defined. LDO's placement photo (TFT at the top) and the manual's p.169/171 insets (labelled Front/Back) are underside views with the front at the top — the printer's left is on the left only if you stand at the *rear* of the inverted machine. Fix: One sentence in 09.3: "Left and right in this chapter are the printer's own, as seen from the front upright. ### F11 · MAJOR · 09-electronics-bay.md:09.12 / 09.13 (also 09.2)
Persona: first-time
Quote: 09.2 "Rear-left corner of the frame: mains WAGO block. Rear centre: the IEC inlet." / 09.12 "Position it rear-centre-left … leave enough room to the left for the WAGO block."
Problem: p.149, p.167 and LDO's placement photo (zoomed, rear-left) all put the inlet panel hard against the rear-left Z-motor mount, with the three WAGOs immediately to its *right* (toward the centre). The inlet segment is one of the three rear skirt segments and Ch 11 builds the rear run outward from it — a panel at the centre, or WAGOs on its left, means a rear skirt that does not close and an inlet to move with the bay full.
Fix: 09.12 Do: "Slide the panel along the extrusion until it touches the rear-left Z-motor mount (p.167)." 09.13: "two M5 T-nuts immediately to the **right** of the inlet panel (LDO photo)". 09.2: "rear-left, in this order from the Z motor: inlet, then WAGO block."

### F12 · MAJOR · 09-electronics-bay.md:09.30
Persona: first-time
Quote: "Slide two M3 T-nuts into the **rear bed extrusion**, hang the probe body under it and fasten with M3×25 SHCS. Slide it left/right so the shaft clears the build plate edge"
Problem: There is no rear bed extrusion — the two bed extrusions run front-to-back (p.28). p.161 bolts the probe to the *side* slot of a bed extrusion at its rear end, two screws horizontal, pin pointing up beside the plate's rear edge; the "1.5" is the horizontal gap between pin and plate edge, and the adjustment is along the extrusion (front/back), not left/right. "Hang under" sends the builder to the wrong face and axis of the wrong extrusion.
Fix: "Two M3 T-nuts into the side slot at the rear end of the bed extrusion under the Z-endstop position (the right-hand one for the 350 config — verify against `home_xy_position` in Ch 12/13); body flat against the extrusion's side, pin up past the plate's rear edge; slide along the extrusion until the pin is ~1.5 mm clear of the plate edge (p.161 detail)." Doable from the bay side while inverted — see F28.

## Chapter verdicts
- **Ch 08 — buildable by a first-timer as written: with fixes.** Weakest steps: 08.11 (latch shuttle never fitted, thumbscrew threads into nothing — F2), 08.35 (mask/diffuser order inverted — F4), 08.1/08.29 (CW2 corner inverted, correct part fails the Check — F3). Extra lens: the SB page images carry the chapter well — the drawings show counts and directions the text confirms — except at p.18 (shuttle) and p.40 (strain relief), where the text contradicts its own image. The probe decision is unmistakable (Read-first, 08.1, 08.50, 08.54, Common mistakes all agree; the Klicky box is scoped and bagged). The Rev D+ connector story is consistent across 08.33/08.46/08.49–52/08.62/08.64, the diagram and 09.25, and every port name, pin and connector type matches `nhsbv2_pcb_pinout.jpg`; the HEF/PCF source conflict is real (board graphic vs adapter silk) and the "wire by port, fix in config" rule is the right call.
- **Ch 09 — buildable by a first-timer as written: with fixes.** Weakest steps: 09.11 (the one sentence that invites energising the inlet — F1), 09.30 (nonexistent "rear bed extrusion", wrong face and axis — F12), 09.12–09.13 (inlet/WAGO order reversed vs p.167 and LDO, rear skirt depends on it — F11). Extra lens: front rail = Leviathan+Pi with USB adapter at the right end, rear rail = SSR left of PSU with terminals facing it — all match LDO's photo and the manual insets, and the PSU/SSR/Leviathan placement is unambiguous once left/right is defined (F10). Mains is kept out as promised except for F1; the jumper-removal-first rule, the bagged cord, and 09.35's staging are solid.

## Praise
- The Nitehawk-SB V2 transcription (08.33, 08.46–08.52, 08.60, 08.62) is accurate to the board image down to pin names, and the fan-pin source conflict is handled the only safe way — wire by port, fix in `printer.cfg`, never re-crimp.
- 08.45 (fan-voltage selectors, verification not modification) and 08.62 ("if it does not drop in, it is backwards — do not press harder") are exactly the two things that kill a V2 toolboard, stated where the hands are.
- The What-you're-looking-at lines earn their space: hinge pin not fastener (08.19), studs not fasteners (08.31), why the shaft must slip in the bearings (08.13), why the SSR control side faces the front rail (09.18).
- Ch 09's discipline holds: every voltage jumper out before the board goes near the rail, nothing wired, cord bagged, meter on the bench, and the PSU selector set and photographed before a duct hides it.
- The Klicky alternative is presented as a bagged option with its cost of switching, not as a temptation.
