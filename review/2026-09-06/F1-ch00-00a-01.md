# F1 read-through — Ch 00, Ch 00a, Ch 01 (Fable, both personas, images opened)

Note: `00-before-you-start.md` Step 00.30 and the 00a links to it were rewritten on disk mid-review (build log → measurement log, no camera). Findings cite step ids, not line numbers. Every image the three chapters rely on exists under `assets/` and was opened; the CAD render `assets/cad/01-15-a.png` comes from the real STEP assembly (`assets/cad/steps.yml` selects `Frame:1/Bottom Extrusions` etc.), so its part positions are authoritative.

### F1 · BLOCKER · 00-before-you-start.md:00.18–00.20
Persona: first-time
Quote: 00.17 "tape each carriage to its rail" → 00.18 "working the carriage back and forth along the rail during the soak" → 00.19 "Slide the carriage until it sits over one of the rail's mounting holes" → 00.20 "Stroke the carriage the full length of the rail several times"
Problem: Three consecutive steps require the carriage to move, which means the tape from 00.17 is off, and none of them says how to keep the carriage from running off the end — the exact failure (spilled balls, scrap rail) the chapter's own Read-first and Common-mistakes warn about. A 13-year-old stroking a carriage "the full length" in a slippery IPA bag will find the end. 00.21 then says "Re-check the carriage tape" as if it had never come off.
Fix: Add to 00.17 (or a new step before 00.18): "Before the soak, replace the carriage tape with two **end stops**: a band of masking tape wrapped tightly round the rail 15 mm from each end (or the plastic end stoppers if your rails shipped with them). The carriage works between the stops for Steps 00.18–00.20 and can never reach the last hole. Rails are handled over the bench, never over the floor, one rail out of its bag at a time." In 00.20 replace "the full length of the rail" with "stop to stop". In 00.21 replace "Re-check the carriage tape" with "Replace the two end stops with one strip of tape across the carriage, then bag".

### F2 · MAJOR · 01-frame.md:01.4 (and 01.10)
Persona: first-time
Quote: "Run each in about half its length, so roughly 8 mm of shank plus the head still stands proud of the end face"
Problem: The verticals' inner faces are fixed 470 mm apart once the bottom square is snug. A top A extrusion whose bolts stand 8 mm proud needs 470 + 2×(8 − slot-lip ≈ 6) ≈ 482 mm between the verticals and will not drop in at Step 01.10; the first-timer has no idea why and the two-person step turns into levering the verticals apart. On the bottom square the same protrusion means each A sits ~6 mm short of the vertical until ~7 turns of bolt pull it in, so 01.6's "corner voids roughly 20 × 20 mm" and 01.7's "push the two A extrusions home" don't happen as described. The head only needs to clear the slot lip (~1.5–2 mm) to be captured in the vertical's channel (manual p.10, right-hand drawing).
Fix: 01.4 Do: "Thread one M5×16 BHCS into the tapped centre bore at **each** end and run it in until only the head plus **about 2 mm of shank** stands proud — just enough for the head to pass the slot lip and sit inside the vertical's channel. Do not leave them half out: the top square must drop between verticals that are already fixed 470 mm apart." 01.4 Check: "…all 16 heads stand proud by the same ~2 mm." 01.10 add: "If a top extrusion will not drop in, its bolts are too far out — run them in further; never spread the verticals."

### F3 · MAJOR · 01-frame.md:Tools, 01.5, 01.7, 01.15
Persona: first-time
Quote: Tools "4 mm **ball-end** hex driver — the ball end is what reaches the blind-joint bolts through the access holes"; 01.7 "Reach in through each access hole with the 4 mm ball-end"; 01.15 "Tighten firmly with the 4 mm hex"
Problem: The frame joints are M5×16 **BHCS** (p.14, Ch 01 hardware table) — a 3 mm hex socket. 4 mm is the M5 SHCS size. Ch 00 Step 00.7 states this correctly ("M5 BHCS 3 mm, M5 SHCS 4 mm… the M5 BHCS the frame uses is a 3 mm drive"), so the two chapters contradict each other and the builder's first act on the frame is a key that won't enter the socket. Also the access hole is coaxial with the bolt (p.10 right, p.15 right — the socket is seen face-on through the hole), so no ball end is needed; a straight key is what you want for the final torque, since ball ends cam out and round sockets under load (Ch 00 Step 00.27 says exactly this).
Fix: Tools: "3 mm hex key, straight, long-arm (kit L-key or the Fabreeko driver) — for the 16 M5×16 BHCS blind joints; the access hole is in line with the bolt, so no ball end is needed and a straight key is better for the torque pass. 3 mm ball-end optional for the first snug." Replace "4 mm" with "3 mm" at 01.7 and 01.15 and in 01.5's Do/Check.

### F4 · MAJOR · 01-frame.md:01.16, 01.18
Persona: first-time
Quote: 01.16 "they butt *between* two opposite bottom rails… `(verify on bench — the manual shows the bracket without a callout for which way up)`"; 01.18 Check "both extrusions resting flat on the rails"
Problem: A bed extrusion is an A (470 mm) and the inner span between the front and rear rails is exactly 470 mm, so there is no room for a bracket leg between the extrusion end and the rail — the only geometry that works, and what the CAD shows (`01-15-a.png`, zoom the purple/orange junction; p.19 inset), is that the bed extrusions **hang one extrusion-width above the rails on the four brackets**: their undersides are level with the rails' top faces, their ends flush with the rails' inner faces, and only the brackets' free legs touch the rails. "Resting flat on the rails" is therefore a Check the builder cannot satisfy, and "which way up" is answerable: the bracket goes on the end face with its free leg pointing **down and outward**. (The deck panel later sits inside the bottom square directly beneath them, which is why it is 469 mm and why Ch 09 bolts the DIN rails up through it into these extrusions.)
Fix: 01.16 Do: "Fit a bracket to each end so that one leg is flat on the end face with its hole over the centre bore (M5×16 into the bore) and the other leg points **down and outward**. When the extrusion is lowered into the frame that free leg lies on top of the front or rear bottom rail, and the bed extrusion hangs from its four brackets with its underside level with the rails' top faces — it does not sit on the rails." Delete the `(verify on bench…)`. 01.18 Check: "Four bolts started, four spacers seated, both extrusions hanging level with their undersides at the rails' top faces and their ends flush with the rails' inner faces; all four bracket legs flat on the rails." Confirm the ~0 mm underside offset once from the STEP (`scripts/cad_render/step_extract.py`, compare `Bed Extrusions` vs `Bottom Extrusions` bounding boxes) and write the number in.

### F5 · MAJOR · 01-frame.md:01.1 Tip, 01.18, 01.19
Persona: first-time
Quote: 01.1 Tip "The frame is symmetric at this stage so any face will do"; 01.18 "each spanning between the two opposite bottom rails"; 01.19 "measure 255 mm in from the outer face of a side extrusion"
Problem: The chapter never says which pair of rails the bed extrusions span. They run **front-to-back** (p.20 dimensions them off "½ printer width"; Ch 03 slides the plate front-to-back along them; Ch 09's DIN rails cross both left-to-right), so FRONT must be one of the two faces their ends land on. A builder who taped FRONT at 01.1, then dropped the bed extrusions between the left and right rails, has a wrong FRONT label, a log with left/right swapped, and Ch 02's Z-rail faces ("front-left faces rear-left") 90° out — four rails to unbolt. Note Ch 02 Step (p.30) says "Put a strip of masking tape on the **front** bed extrusion", which has no meaning if they run front-to-back — a cross-chapter conflict for the Ch 02 reviewer.
Fix: 01.1 Tip: "…any face will do **until Step 01.18**: the bed extrusions run front-to-back, so FRONT must be one of the two faces their ends land on." 01.18 Do: "The bed extrusions run **front-to-back**: their bracketed ends land on the **front and rear** bottom rails, and they sit left and right of the printer centreline. If your FRONT tape is on a face parallel to them, move it now — Ch 02, 03 and 09 all assume this." 01.19: "…from the outer face of the **left (or right) side rail**, across the front rail; mark it on the front and rear rails."

### F6 · MAJOR · 01-frame.md:01.1 (and 00-before-you-start.md:00.10)
Persona: first-time / second-time
Quote: 01.1 Check "No feeler you can slip under the straightedge anywhere in the working area"; 00.10 "record the largest leaf that goes under anywhere… no published tolerance exists"
Problem: The same test is run twice with different pass criteria: 00.10 records a gap and passes, 01.1 demands none. A 0.02–0.05 mm leaf will pass somewhere under a 600 mm straightedge on any real kitchen slab, so a first-timer fails Step 01.1's Check on day one with no recovery path — and the check is theatre anyway, because 2020 extrusion straightness tolerance (≈0.5 mm/m per the usual HFS5 spec — cite the vendor) is an order of magnitude larger than the counter's error, and a symmetric dish leaves the four corner contact points coplanar (see F21).
Fix: Make 01.1 a one-line re-verification of the patch masked at 00.10 ("re-clean, one pass of the straightedge in five positions, worst gap no larger than you recorded at 00.10") and give 00.10 a stated acceptance: "record the worst leaf; ≤ 0.1 mm across the patch is finer than the extrusions themselves — proceed; a leaf ≥ 0.3 mm, or any rock of the straightedge, means choose another patch or another surface. Twist (one corner high) matters, dish does not." Log entry in 01.22 changes from "none passes" to the recorded number.

### F7 · MAJOR · 00-before-you-start.md:chapter structure (00.8, 00.9, 00.13–00.16, 00.23–00.29, 00.31, 00.32)
Persona: first-time
Quote: Prerequisites "The kit itself, unopened"; 00.9 Check "Grease and IPA are on the bench before you touch a rail"; 00.31 "Join all three before the kit arrives"; 00.32 "Post them today"

### F21 · MINOR · 01-frame.md:01.1, Common mistakes
Persona: second-time
Quote: "any dish or crown in the counter is copied permanently into the printer"; "A 0.5 mm dish across 500 mm becomes a twisted frame"
Problem: A dish or crown leaves the four corner contact points coplanar — it cannot twist a frame resting on its corners (a crown can make it rock about the ridge). Twist comes from a **twisted** surface (one corner high). The 0.5 mm is an invented figure.
Fix: "…any **twist** in the counter — one corner high — is copied permanently into the printer; a crown shows up as rock." Common mistakes: "A surface with one corner a few tenths high becomes a twisted frame that reads square on every diagonal and still racks the gantry."

## Chapter verdicts
- **Ch 00 — buildable by a first-timer as written: with fixes.** Kit-day work is finishable before the frame (~3.5 h of Pause segments) only if the pre-kit half was done weeks earlier; the chapter doesn't separate the two (F7). It is mostly good at saying what not to open (00.1, 00.3, 00.4, 00.12), missing only rails/boards at 00.3 (F17). Weakest steps: 00.18–00.20 (carriage safety, F1), 00.22 + Prerequisites (B00 gate needs the kit, F8), 00.14/00.18 (image–text mismatches, F10/F11).
- **Ch 00a — buildable (readable) by a first-timer as written: yes, with the 00a.2 fix.** Weakest steps: 00a.2 (room-rule contradiction, F9), 00a.11 (power-on count, F23), 00a.4 (lead-test sentence, F24). The images match the text; the Rev C SSR photo is correctly flagged.
- **Ch 01 — buildable by a first-timer as written: with fixes.** Could they square the frame on the kitchen counter from these steps alone? The squaring procedure itself (01.9–01.14, 01.20, the log table) is followable; three things stop them before they get there: the half-out bolts (F2), the 4 mm driver (F3) and the zero-feeler flatness check (F6), plus the bed-extrusion geometry they cannot satisfy (F4) and the FRONT ambiguity that bites in Ch 02 (F5). Weakest steps: 01.4, 01.16/01.18, 01.1.

## Praise
- The `What you're looking at` lines are consistently the best sentence in each step — 01.5 and 01.7's explanation of why the access holes face outward is exactly what a first-timer needs before the first corner.
- The Pause discipline is honest: 01.5's warning that the next 65 minutes cannot be broken, and 00.18's "genuine wait state", are the kind of thing only a careful author writes.
- 01.2's Check (B extrusions are the only ones with access holes) is a perfect one-glance identification — verified against p.13.
- 01.12's "same tool hooked on the same feature; relative equality is the measurement" and the 01.22 log table make the squareness check reproducible rather than vibes.
- 00a turns safety into artefacts (a posted rule, a labelled breaker, a proved meter, five agreed responses) instead of warnings — keep that structure.
