### G1 · MAJOR · 14-calibration.md:Step 14.4 (and 14.5; 13.25 same pattern)
Quote: "Machine cold or cooling, door open, `M84` so the gantry moves by hand. Move the X extrusion forward until the X/Y idler centres are 150 mm from the front idler centres … Repeat on B … go back and forth"
Problem: `M84` releases all seven motors, the four Z motors included. The manual's own Ch 06b.1 says what happens next — "with the Z motors released the gantry simply falls" — and 06b.5 disables *only* `stepper_x`/`stepper_y` for exactly this reason. 14.4 has the reader spend 10–20 min plucking belts with the gantry mid-travel and unpowered; it sinks onto the plate (nozzle first). 14.5 then says "Move the gantry up" with nothing homed and no idle-timeout raise, so a 4-belt pass longer than the config's 1800 s timeout drops it again mid-pluck.
Fix: 14.4 Do → "`SET_IDLE_TIMEOUT TIMEOUT=99999`, `G28`, jog the gantry to mid-height, then `SET_STEPPER_ENABLE STEPPER=stepper_x ENABLE=0` and the same for `stepper_y` — only those two; the Z motors stay energised and hold the gantry (Ch 06b Steps 06b.1, 06b.5). Never `M84` on this machine with the gantry in the air." 14.5 Do → "Z motors still holding, timeout still raised: jog the gantry up until …". Add to the 14.6 Pause or 14.5 Check: "the `RESTART` at 14.6 restores the idle timeout." 13.25: replace `M84` with the two `SET_STEPPER_ENABLE … ENABLE=0` lines (the gantry is 50 mm up after five z_hops; only A/B need to be free). 13.17 is the one legitimate `M84` (the gantry has to be moved in Z by hand) — leave it, but add "hold the gantry; it will sink once the Z motors are off".

### G2 · MAJOR · 13-initial-startup.md:Step 13.41 (Check) — PrusaSlicer will prepend its own `M104`/`M190`
Quote: "**Check:** … the G-code preview's first lines are your `PRINT_START BED=110 EXTRUDER=260 CHAMBER=0` with **no** `M190`/`M109` before it — if the slicer added its own heating, the flavour or the start G-code is wrong."
Problem: Every PrusaResearch printer preset (the copied `Prusa CORE One HF0.4 nozzle` included) ships `autoemit_temperature_commands = 1` — Printer Settings → Custom G-code → **Emit temperature commands automatically** (present since 2.6; string table `ps296.pot` L4108 / `ps300.pot` L3774). With that ticked and a start G-code that contains none of M104/M109/M140/M190, the slicer inserts `M104 S260` and `M190 S110` *before* `PRINT_START`. Neither the Klipper flavour nor the start G-code is "wrong", so the reader cannot pass the Check and has no remedy. If they print anyway, the hotend is at 260 °C oozing while Ch 12's cold `G28` homes Z on the nozzle-probe pin — Z=0 taken through a blob of ASA.
Fix: in the *Printer profile* paragraph, after the End G-code: "**Untick Emit temperature commands automatically** (top of the Custom G-code page). Leave it ticked and PrusaSlicer inserts `M104 S260` / `M190 S110` ahead of `PRINT_START`, and the nozzle homes on the pin at 260 °C." Check → "… with no `M104`/`M190` before it — if there is, the Emit-temperature-commands box is still ticked."

### G3 · MAJOR · 14-calibration.md:Step 14.8 (also 13.31, 13.35, 14.3 in a lesser way)
Quote: 14.8: "Expect **30–45 minutes** from cold to a stable chamber; that is the soak … **Check:** With bed at 110 °C, panels on and door closed, `chamber_temp` … climbs past 45 °C within ~20 minutes and settles … If it stalls below 45 °C, look for a missing panel, an open keystone blank, or the exhaust left open."
Problem: The LDO config ships `[idle_timeout] timeout: 1800` (L455–456). Klipper's `idle_timeout.py` (`check_idle_timeout`, L60–72) looks only at toolhead print time and the gcode mutex — a heater holding a target is *not* activity — and its default script is `TURN_OFF_HEATERS` + `M84`. A 14.8 soak done as written (set the bed from the UI, watch the graph) has its heaters switched off and the Z motors released 30 minutes after the last move; the chamber then "stalls below 45 °C" and the reader goes hunting for a missing panel, while the gantry drops (G1). 14.6 is safe only because it raises the timeout for the Z motors' sake and never says the heaters were also at risk. 13.31/13.35/14.3 (10–20 min) fit inside 30 min, but 13.32's "wait another 5 minutes and repeat" and 13.35's "soak for the time you recorded" can run past it, and nothing tells the reader why the bed went off. `PRINT_START` is safe (`G4` advances print time; `TEMPERATURE_WAIT`/`M190` hold the mutex).
Fix: 14.8 Do, first line: "`SET_IDLE_TIMEOUT TIMEOUT=7200` — the LDO config's 1800 s idle timeout turns the heaters **and the Z motors** off after 30 min without a move; a static soak is exactly that. `RESTART`, `FIRMWARE_RESTART` and every `SAVE_CONFIG` put it back to 1800." Same one-liner at 13.31 (and let 13.35/14.3 reference it). Ch 13 What-if row: "Bed and hotend switched themselves off mid-soak, motors released | `[idle_timeout] timeout: 1800` (LDO config), not a fault | `SET_IDLE_TIMEOUT TIMEOUT=7200` before a soak; re-heat".

### G5 · MINOR · 13-initial-startup.md:Tools, Read first #6, Step 13.22 (Source), Common mistakes, What-if · 14-calibration.md:Tools
Quote: "`RESTART` is **not** a stop: Klipper only pulls `M112` out of the queue while a move is executing" · 13.22 Source: "[Klipper `gcode.py`] (M112 is scanned out of the pending input while a command runs)"
Problem: The mechanism cited is the pseudo-tty input path (`gcode.py` L456–464), which Moonraker never uses. Through Mainsail a console line goes to `gcode/script` → `run_script` → `with self.mutex` (`gcode.py` L239–240, `webhooks.py` L448) — that *does* queue behind a running `G28`. What makes the typed `M112` work is Mainsail itself: `src/store/printer/actions.ts` L163–164 turns a line that is exactly `M112` (trimmed, case-insensitive) into `printer.emergency_stop`, i.e. klippy's `emergency_stop` endpoint → `invoke_shutdown` with no mutex (`webhooks.py` L382–383) — the same call as the red button. So the instruction is right, the reason is wrong, and there is one trap: `M112` with anything else on the line (`M112 ; stop`, or `M112` pasted after another command) is an ordinary queued command.
Fix: "Type `M112` and nothing else on the line. Mainsail turns exactly that into the same emergency-stop call as its red button (and KlipperScreen's E-stop), which bypasses the command queue; `RESTART`, or `M112` with a comment or a second command on the line, waits its turn behind the move." Re-source 13.22 to Mainsail `actions.ts` + klippy `webhooks.py`; keep `FIRMWARE_RESTART` afterwards.
