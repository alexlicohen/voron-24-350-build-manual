# F2 — Read-through: Ch 02 (Z drives) and Ch 03 (Build plate)

Evidence base: every manual page p.22–61 read as PNG; LDO build-FAQ and 350 Rev D BOM fetched raw (curl); the Z_Drive / Z_Idlers / Panel_Mounting STLs sliced for hole geometry (`scratchpad/holes.py`, output `holes-a.txt`/`holes-b.txt`); the official STEP cache (`scratchpad/cad/cache`) queried for which corner holds which subassembly and chirality-matched against the STL hands (`scratchpad/chiral.py`); plan-view renders of every a/b pair in `scratchpad/ortho/`. Nothing in the project was edited.

Ground truth the findings rest on (all verified, see "Verified OK"):
- STL geometry @ de7e89d: `z_drive_retainer` = the shallow half (16.2 mm), six Ø4.7 insert pockets in its flat print face + one in a side face. `z_drive_main` = the deep half (26.8 mm), six Ø6×3 mm **counterbores** (screw heads) in its flat print face, two Ø4.7 insert pockets in one side face, two Ø5.4×35 mm M5 through-holes with Ø9×24 mm head wells — the only M5×40-length holes in the chapter. `z_motor_mount` foot: two Ø11 counterbores over Ø5.4×1.5 mm — M5×10 holes. `[a]_z_drive_baseplate`: three M3 counterbored holes + one M5 hole (foot screw). `_b` is the Y-mirror of `_a` for every pair.
- CAD (Voron_2.4r2_Assembly.step @ de7e89d): "Z Belt Drive A" sits at front-left (Z0) and rear-right (Z2); "B" at rear-left (Z1) and front-right (Z3). Same for "Z Idler A/B". STL `_a` matches CAD "A" (chamfer 0.06–0.2 mm vs 1.2–4.6 mm for the other hand) for motor mounts, baseplates and idler brackets.
- CAD Base Plate subassembly = plate + M5 hex nut + M5×16 BHCS + 3× M3×8: the captive nut is the rubber foot's thread.
- Manual p.41: two M5×40 go down through the assembled **drive** (baseplate disc visible); p.42: the **motor** assembly is the part slid in at an angle, one M5×10 into its foot, "DON'T TIGHTEN"; p.44: the cam pushes the motor; p.45: the two M5×10 are tightened, foot with M5×16.

### F1 · BLOCKER · 02-z-drives.md:02.30–02.35 (and 02.37 Pause, Checkpoint)
Persona: first-time
Quote: 02.31 "drive two M5×40 SHCS down through the counterbored holes in the mount's foot" / 02.32 "Insert the drive body at an angle … Fit one M5×10 BHCS through the drive body's foot"
Problem: Drive and motor are swapped against p.41–45 and against the parts. The motor mount's foot holes are 9 mm deep M5×10 holes: an M5×40 through them bottoms in the extrusion slot ~25 mm early and jacks the T-nut. The drive has no "foot" for an M5×10 — its two mounting holes are 35 mm deep wells. 02.34's physics is also inverted (the cam pushes the **motor**, not the drive), so a builder who "leaves the drive loose" and tightens the motor gets a slack loop and a drive that skews when the cam closes.
Fix: Replace 02.30–02.36 Do/Check with:
- 02.30 Do: "Slide four M5 T-nuts into the two bottom extrusions meeting at this corner, two in each: the pair in the extrusion the drive body sits on take the drive's two M5×40; the pair in the extrusion the motor sits on take the motor foot's M5×10 and the cam's M5×10 (p.41)."
- 02.31 title "Bolt the drive body down with two M5×40". Do: "Set the finished drive into the corner, baseplate up (the printer is inverted, so the foot face points at the ceiling), belt window toward the extrusion the motor will sit on. Drop the two M5×40 SHCS down the two deep wells in the drive body — one well is inside the baseplate's C-notch, the other beside the baseplate — and run them into the T-nuts. Snug so the body sits hard in the corner; final tightening is 02.35." Check: "Body against both extrusions; the 188 mm loop hanging out of the belt window over the motor's extrusion."
- 02.32 title "Slide the motor assembly in at an angle". Do: "Hold the motor/mount at an angle, hook the hanging loop over the 16T, and slide the mount's foot down onto the extrusion beside the drive (p.42). One M5×10 BHCS through the foot hole nearest the drive into its T-nut. Leave it loose — the cam has to move the motor."
- 02.33: cam goes on the extrusion at the far end of the motor foot; Check "cam free to rotate; **motor** free to slide".
- 02.34 Do: "Rotate the cam closed (arrow, p.44). It pushes the motor foot away from the drive body and tensions the loop."
- 02.35 Do: "Tighten the two M5×10 (motor foot, cam), then re-check the two M5×40 are tight."
- 02.36 Do: "…one M5×16 BHCS into the M5 nut you dropped into the baseplate at 02.25."
- Pause after 02.38: "never with a cam open and the **motor** free to slide". Checkpoint line "no drive shifted when its cam closed" is still right (p.46).

### F2 · BLOCKER · 02-z-drives.md:02.22, 02.24 (also 02.03, 02.23)
Persona: first-time
Quote: 02.22 "Seat the shaft assembly into `z_drive_main`" / 02.24 "Lay the retainer over the drive body … drive the six M3×40 SHCS through the stack into the heat-set inserts"
Problem: The halves are named the wrong way round and the screw direction is never stated. Per the STL the M3×40 heads sit in the six counterbores on the **main**'s flat face and the tips bite the inserts on the **retainer**'s flat face; manual p.35/36 draws exactly that (shaft into the shallow tray = retainer; the blue lid with six counterbored holes and the two M5 wells on its side = main). Following the chapter, the shaft goes into the deep half, the retainer goes on top inserts-up, and the builder starts six M3×40 into the visible brass inserts from above: the screw threads only in the top part's own insert, clamps nothing, and tightening pulls the insert toward the head — out of the ASA boss. Rework at best, a scrapped retainer at worst.
Fix: 02.22 title "Seat the shaft assembly into `z_drive_retainer` (the shallow half)"; Do: "Put the retainer print-face down — that is the half with six brass inserts in its flat face; inserts down, cradles up. Lower the shaft assembly so each 625 drops into a cradle and the loop lies in the pocket." 02.24 title "Close the drive with `z_drive_main` and six M3×40"; Do: "Lower the main (the deeper half — its flat face has six counterbored holes and no inserts) over the shaft, counterbores up, until the two flat faces meet. Drive the six M3×40 down through the counterbores; they pass through both halves and bite the retainer's inserts for the last ~3 mm. Every head must sink into a counterbore — if a head is sitting on a brass ring you have the halves upside down: stop and back it out." 02.03 add: "The six Ø6 counterbores on the main's flat face are for screw heads — no insert goes there." Fix the What-you're-looking-at lines to match (main = deep half with belt window + M5 wells; retainer = shallow tray).

### F3 · BLOCKER · 03-build-plate.md:03.9
Persona: first-time
Quote: "Peel the backing from one edge only, align that edge and the two adjacent corners, then lower the pad progressively across the plate"
Problem: One-shot lamination with no positive alignment: an exposed adhesive edge cannot be slid into position, the sheet size relative to the plate is never stated (LDO BOM gives no dimension — "even border all round" may not exist if the pad is 355 mm), which face is adhesive is not said, and there is no "if the first strip lands crooked" instruction. A crooked or bubbled sheet is a lost part and a multi-day delay; this is the step the extra lens asked about and it is not yet unmistakable.
Fix: Do: "Measure the pad against the plate before peeling anything (verify on bench — LDO publishes no drawing): same 355 mm as the plate → edge-flush to the **back** edge and one side; smaller → centre it and write the border down. The liner side is the adhesive; the plain rubber face is the magnet. The four bolt holes are not pre-cut (that is 03.10). Hinge it: with the liner on, lay the pad exactly where it goes and tape it along the back edge with two strips of masking tape running from the pad's top face over the plate's side — that tape is the hinge. Fold the pad back over the hinge, peel 40–50 mm of liner at the hinged edge and cut it off, lay that strip down and squeegee it. Then pull the liner out underneath while squeegeeing from the hinge toward you, ~50 mm per stroke, keeping the unlaid pad lifted so adhesive never touches ahead of the squeegee. Remove the hinge tape and press the whole sheet with a card edge or roller (p.54). If the first strip lands crooked, lift it **immediately** — once more than a few cm are down it will not reposition." Add masking tape to Ch 03 Tools.

### F4 · MAJOR · 02-z-drives.md:02.01, 02.29, 02.38, 02.43 (+ pair renders)
Persona: first-time
Quote: 02.01 "hold two candidates the same way up, see which side the bearing cradles open toward, and write the letter on the part" / 02.38 "Repeat … at the opposing corner with the same hand of parts"
Problem: A first-timer cannot assign the letters: the pair renders are dark isometrics from one camera, the "cradles open toward" test has no reference side, the chapter never says which corner takes `a`, and "opposing corner" reads as either diagonal (right) or the other front corner (wrong — adjacent corners are mirrors). A wrong-hand drive bolts on fine and is only discovered at Ch 07 when the 20T is not under the idler.
Fix: (1) State the map once in 02.02 and repeat in 02.29/02.38/02.43: "`_a` parts build the **front-left (Z0)** and **rear-right (Z2)** corners; `_b` parts build **rear-left (Z1)** and **front-right (Z3)**. Diagonal corners share a hand; neighbours are mirrors" (CAD Z Belt Drive A/B and Z Idler A/B positions, chirality-matched to the STL hands). Change "opposing corner" → "diagonally opposite corner (Z2)". (2) Replace the six isometric pair renders with **plan-view** pairs (looking straight down on the part as it sits on the bench, print face down; `render_parts.py` with `view_init(90,-90)`; my versions are in `scratchpad/ortho/pair-*-top.png`) — a mirror pair is obvious in plan and impossible in isometric. (3) Give the bench test in words for the parts handled first: main — "print face down, cradles up, shaft line left–right, rectangular belt window in the right half: the bitten-off corner and the side face with two big 9 mm bolt wells are on the FAR side for `_a`, toward you for `_b`"; retainer — "inserts down, cradles up, rectangular window right / trapezoid window left: bite NEAR-left = `_a`, FAR-left = `_b` (flipped onto its main, the bites coincide)"; motor mount — "stood on its print face, thick foot arm (two large counterbored holes) on your right running toward you: the thin motor plate is the FAR edge on `_a`, the NEAR edge on `_b`"; baseplate — "counterbored face up, C-notch opening to your left: far-left = `_a`, near-left = `_b`"; cam — "flat face down, round lobe on your left: the upright tab is at the far-right on `_a`, near-right on `_b`". (4) Upstream: the B01/B02 print chapters should have the parts lettered as they come off the plate; 02.01 then only verifies.

### F5 · MAJOR · 02-z-drives.md:02.13 ↔ 02.14 (and 02.38)
Persona: first-time
Quote: 02.13 "Slide the eight clips into the extrusion slots that the deck panel will rest on, spaced evenly along the unsupported spans"
Problem: Wrong order and no geometry. The CAD puts the deck panel *inside* the bottom frame's opening (369 in a 370 opening; 469/470 on the 350), hanging under the bed extrusions; `deck_support_3mm` is a 20×14.5×5.8 mm T-tab that twists into the bottom-frame extrusions' inward-facing slot with a ~9.5 mm ledge under the panel edge. Fitted before the panel (inverted, the ledges then sit above the panel's plane) they block the panel from dropping in; the builder pulls all eight out again. LDO's note is at p.29–30, after the panel. Nothing says which extrusion, which slot, or that these clips are the only thing holding the panel when the printer is turned upright at 02.38 (the four M5×10 are deferred to Ch 09).
Fix: Swap the steps: 02.13 = panel in (add "it drops inside the bottom frame's opening and rests on the two bed extrusions, flush with the frame face"), 02.14 = clips. Clip Do: "Each clip is a T-tab with a ledge. Turn the tab end-on, push it into the inward-facing slot of a bottom-frame extrusion just above the panel edge, and twist it 90° so the tab locks in the slot and the ledge lies over the panel edge — two per side, evenly spaced (spacing not specified by Voron or LDO). When the printer is turned upright these eight ledges are what carry the panel until Ch 09 bolts it." Check: "Eight ledges over the panel edge; the panel cannot be lifted out." Add to 02.38 Check: "confirm the deck is captured by its clips before you turn the frame over." (verify with one clip in hand — geometry from the STL)

## Chapter verdicts
- **Ch 02 — buildable by a first-timer as written: no.** Weakest: 02.31/02.32 (drive and motor swapped — the M5×40 will not fit where the text sends it), 02.22/02.24 (halves mis-named, M3×40 direction unstated — insert pull-out), 02.01/02.38 (no way to assign `a`/`b`, no corner map, "opposing corner"). With F1–F9 applied: yes.
- **Ch 03 — buildable by a first-timer as written: with fixes.** Weakest: 03.9 (lamination alignment method), 03.11 (unexplained 25 mm), 03.4 (pad screws "snug" vs LDO's do-not-overtighten).

## Praise
- 02.09's CAD-verified rail-face correction, with the plan-view render, is exactly the standard the rest of the chapter should meet.
- 02.19 stack order and count (bearing · 20T · 2 spacers · bearing · 2 spacers · 80T · bearing; 4 spacers/drive) match p.33 and the CAD to the shim.
- 02.03's 36 = 7/retainer + 2/main and the Ø4.7 pocket test are exactly right per the STL; "trust the part" is the right instruction.
- Ch 03 sequencing is sound: skip-pages handled as inspections, trim-before-bolts (03.10), one-bolt rule (03.17), and the Pause lines keep the lamination in one sitting.
- The 16T isolation warnings (header, 02.27 Tip, Common mistakes) and the "measure the deck, do not resolve it from documents" call are the kind of concrete pre-emption a first-timer needs.
